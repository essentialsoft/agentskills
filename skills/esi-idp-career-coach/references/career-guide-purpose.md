# ESI Career Guide Purpose and Interpretation

## Definition

An ESI Career Guide is the authoritative standard for role-level excellence across a discipline. It defines observable behaviors, technical competencies, and alignment to ESI Leadership Principles for each level.

It is not a job description. A job description describes a particular opening at a particular moment; a Career Guide is a persistent discipline-wide standard.

## Intended Uses

- Anchor role outcomes and hiring expectations.
- Set performance-accountability expectations before employment begins.
- Interpret observations against stable level expectations.
- Ground IDPs in the gap between current evidence and current- or next-level mastery.
- Make advancement evidence legible and defensible.
- Enable consistent comparisons across teams, managers, and disciplines.

## What It Is Not

- A checklist to complete.
- A performance-review form.
- A substitute for MOST, IDP, or alignment processes.
- A direct compensation table.
- Proof that tenure grants access to a level.
- A guarantee that one goal or accomplishment results in promotion.

## Core Design Principle

Career Guides operate on competency over tenure. The relevant question is how well someone performs and at what scope, not how long the person has been doing the work. Experience bands may provide context but do not determine readiness.

The guide defines the ceiling of a level. Promotion readiness requires sustained evidence across a majority of applicable signal categories, not a single exceptional moment.

## How to Use the CSV

The file [esi-career-guides.csv](esi-career-guides.csv) contains Software Engineer IC1–IC6 expectations, IC promotion signals, People Leadership responsibilities, and People Leadership promotion signals.

For a level comparison:

1. Identify the correct track and the employee's current level.
2. Read the current-level column for the relevant competency or principle.
3. Read the adjacent next-level column only when next-level development is in scope.
4. Describe the observable change in behavior, scope, independence, influence, quality, or impact.
5. Ask for evidence showing where the employee currently operates.
6. Avoid converting every row into a checklist or requiring every goal to be promotional.

Useful CSV row labels include:

- Level Summary
- ESI Leadership Principles
- Code Quality & Reliability
- Architecture & Design
- Execution, Delivery & Impact
- Operations & Production Ownership
- AI Fluency & Applied Automation
- Communication, Alignment
- Software Engineer — IC Promotion Signals
- Software Engineer — People Leadership Responsibilities
- PL People Leadership — Promotion Signals

## Leadership Principle Selection

Select only two or three principles whose behaviors are demonstrated by the goal's planned actions. State the action-to-principle connection. Use the exact canonical names from the guide.

Do not infer a principle merely because it sounds positive or generally relevant.
