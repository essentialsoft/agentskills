# ESI IDP Manager-Coaching Rubric

## Purpose

The IDP process develops the manager as well as the employee. Apply a consistent company-wide quality bar while helping the manager understand and explain why a goal needs work. The outcome is a coaching conversation and a finalized revised IDP, not a corrected document forwarded without discussion.

## Five Required Dimensions

### What to Develop

Require one specific skill, behavior, competency, or deliverable.

Flag:

- open-ended aspirations;
- restatements of a job description or standing responsibility;
- subjective wording such as “get better” or “be more proactive”;
- multiple unrelated objectives combined into one goal;
- scope too broad to inspect by year-end.

### Current Proficiency

Require a specific diagnosis of:

- what the employee can do today;
- what is not yet consistent;
- where or when the gap appears;
- why the gap exists, when known;
- evidence or examples supporting the assessment.

Reject:

- single-word labels such as beginner or intermediate;
- vague claims such as “I do okay” or “sometimes active”;
- status updates disguised as proficiency;
- a job-description restatement;
- blank, None, or Not applicable responses.

Do not invent evidence. Ask what observations would establish the diagnosis when evidence is missing.

### How to Develop It

Require an actual development system with:

- a trigger or cadence;
- concrete practice activities;
- a real artifact or observable behavior;
- a validation or feedback step;
- a measurable minimum expressed as a count, frequency, percentage, quality threshold, or other checkable target;
- a target date or bounded milestone.

Intentions such as “try more,” “continue to,” or “work on” are insufficient without observable actions and measures.

### Support Needed from Manager

Require a specific, proactive manager commitment, such as:

- observing work;
- giving scheduled feedback;
- reviewing an artifact;
- coaching before or after an activity;
- providing access, introductions, opportunities, or prioritization help;
- removing a blocker.

Blank, N/A, and None are never acceptable. If the employee initially believes no support is needed, identify the smallest useful coaching or validation commitment.

### Leadership Principles Involved

Select only two or three canonical ESI Leadership Principles directly demonstrated by planned actions. Explain the action-to-principle trace.

Canonical names:

- Customer Obsession
- Innovate and Simplify
- Exercise Good Judgment
- Commit to Learning
- Cultivate Talent
- Demand Excellence
- Think Boldly
- Act with Urgency
- Earn Trust
- Do More with Less
- Go Deeper
- Challenge and Commit
- Deliver Outcomes
- Empower Others

Flag invented, obsolete, misspelled, excessive, or loosely related principle names. When intent is clear, suggest the canonical replacement; for example, “Deliver Results” should be checked against “Deliver Outcomes.”

## Year-End Scope

Every goal must be achievable or meaningfully progressable within the remainder of the current calendar year.

Always flag:

- “continue to” or “ongoing” language;
- missing target dates;
- standing responsibilities presented as development goals;
- plans that cannot produce meaningful evidence by year-end;
- several major deliverables bundled into one goal.

For longer-term development, define the bounded outcome for the current cycle and identify what may carry into the next.

Ask: “Is this achievable by year-end, and if not, what is the realistic bounded version for this cycle?”

## Mandatory Red Flags

- Support Needed is blank, N/A, or None.
- Current Proficiency is a label, generic claim, status update, or job-description restatement.
- No measurable minimum, validation method, or target date.
- The goal bundles multiple distinct deliverables.
- Leadership Principles do not trace to real actions, do not match current canonical principles, or exceed two or three without justification.
- Manager/employee alignment notes or check-in cadence are blank when those fields are present.
- A review or approval claim cannot be verified.
- Success depends on subjective language that cannot be checked later.

## Manager Process

1. Review the feedback in full before meeting with the employee.
2. Meet directly; do not merely forward a corrected document.
3. Explain the reasoning behind the changes.
4. Push for written specificity that an outside reader can understand.
5. Use judgment in framing the guidance while maintaining the substantive standard.
6. End with a manager- and employee-confirmed revised IDP and check-in cadence.

## Tone

- Constructive, not punitive.
- Direct and concrete.
- Consistent across teams and seniority.
- Focused on objective, verifiable development.
- Open to reasoned manager disagreement when team context warrants it.
